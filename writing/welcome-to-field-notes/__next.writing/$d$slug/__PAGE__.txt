<h2>Topics I cover</h2>
<p>The archive focuses on work I can test and explain:</p>
<ul>
<li>embedded systems, firmware, sensors, and connected-device architecture;</li>
<li>Linux, networking, and legal cybersecurity challenges;</li>
<li>Flutter interfaces for physical products;</li>
<li>robotics and control systems;</li>
<li>AI and machine-learning experiments with reproducible inputs and results.</li>
</ul>
<p>Some entries are short references. Longer walkthroughs include the environment,
commands, expected output, failure modes, and files needed to repeat the work.</p>
<h2>How I write a walkthrough</h2>
<p>A walkthrough should help the reader recognize the problem again. My longer
articles follow a consistent structure:</p>
<ol>
<li>define the goal and constraints;</li>
<li>record the environment and prerequisites;</li>
<li>show the investigation or design process;</li>
<li>explain the working solution;</li>
<li>document common failure modes;</li>
<li>finish with reusable checks or lessons.</li>
</ol>
<blockquote>
<p>A useful technical note gives the reader a method they can apply elsewhere.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2>Reproducible examples</h2>
<p>Commands appear in copyable terminal blocks:</p>
<pre><code class="language-bash">ssh user@example-host -p 2220
</code></pre>
<p>I record versions, dependencies, configuration, and expected output when they
change the result. Security articles stay within legal labs, owned systems, and
defensive learning.</p>
<h2>Series in progress</h2>
<p>The OverTheWire Bandit walkthrough covers Linux commands and problem-solving in
a controlled challenge environment. The Learn With Me series develops practical
foundations in Linux, Python, Dart and Flutter, and Git.</p>
<p>The archive grows alongside active engineering and teaching work. If an entry
helps another person verify a result or avoid a repeated mistake, it has served
its purpose.</p>
